Consensus you can break.

raftkv is a sharded key-value store built on a from-scratch implementation of the Raft consensus algorithm in Go. The cluster on this page is real. Crash any node and watch the others elect a new leader, without losing a single committed write.

00  /  In plain words

A database that survives crashes.

Most apps keep their data on one computer. If it crashes, the app goes down, and if its disk dies, the data is gone. raftkv keeps every piece of data on three computers that vote on every change, so any one of them can fail at any moment and nothing is lost.

  1. Shard leaders (top left). The data is split into three slices, and each slice has one machine in charge. When machines were crashed, node 3 took charge of all three slices.
  2. Requests per second (top right). The yellow line is writes that succeeded. It kept going while machines failed. The small blue line is the few that arrived mid-handover.
  3. Response time (bottom left). The slowest requests briefly took 220 ms during a handover, then fell back on their own. Nobody restarted anything.
  4. Elections (bottom right). Each step up is the machines voting in a new leader after one failed. Result: 29,463 writes succeeded (95.5%), and no saved data was lost.

    02  /  What you're watching

    Four rules keep the data safe.

    Raft turns three unreliable machines into one reliable log. These are the mechanisms behind every pulse and ripple in the scene above.

    01

    Heartbeats

    The leader pings every follower every 50 ms. If a follower hears nothing for 300 to 600 ms, it assumes the leader is gone.

    02

    Election

    A follower raises the term and asks for votes. Only a candidate whose log is up to date can win a majority, so a new leader always has every committed write.

    03

    Replication

    The leader appends each write to its log and copies it to followers. Once 2 of 3 have it on disk, it is committed and can never be lost.

    04

    Recovery

    A crashed node restarts from its write-ahead log on disk, then receives the entries it missed, or a snapshot if it fell too far behind.
